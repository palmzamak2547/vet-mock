# 5.227.2: a screen opened on a cold visit no longer waits for the whole year's questions

A cold visit asks for the selected year's question banks at boot: 74 files and 2.6 MB gzipped for year 5. They were
asked for all at once, and a screen opened meanwhile waited behind every one of them for its own chunk. The loader
(`src/data/questions.js`) now fetches four banks at a time through one shared limiter, so a chunk asked for later
waits behind four. Every bank still arrives, in the same order, merged once; a failed bank still fails the load and
keeps its retry. Nothing on any screen changes except when it appears.

## Measured

Production build, headless Chromium at 390x844 @3x, CPU slowed 4x, Lighthouse's slow 4G (150 ms, 1.6 Mbps), a
year-5 student, median of 3 cold visits, `vite preview` (HTTP/1.1). "Ready" is the first frame where `#main` names the
screen and nothing in it says กำลังโหลด.

| screen, cold visit | ready before | ready after |
|---|---|---|
| Progress (/app/progress) | 17.3 s | 4.4 s |
| Question manager (/app/questions) | 16.9 s | 4.2 s |
| Clips (/app/videos) | 19.6 s | 7.5 s |
| VetWiki article (/wiki/com5/rabies) | 20.5 s | 9.6 s |
| Home | 6.1 s | 5.8 s |
| Study list (/app/study) | 3.6 s | 4.0 s |
| Review (/app/review) | 4.0 s | 4.1 s |

The banks finish at the same time as before (the visit's network goes quiet after 19.6 to 23.1 s in both builds), so
counts and practice sets are ready when they were. The full table, the method and what could not be measured:
`C:/dev/vetmock-hunt/ui/SPEED-20261115.md` (outside the repository).

## Guard

`tests/unit/bank-fetch-limit.test.mjs`: at most `BANK_FETCHES_AT_ONCE` banks in flight over 74, started in order,
every bank returned in order; a failed bank fails the load and the queue behind it still settles; a task that throws
frees its place; `_runLoaders` fetches only through `importBanks` and its shared limiter.

## Tried and taken back

PF-06 (every PDF page asked of pdf.js on open): rows sized from page 1 and asking for their own page only near the
screen. A 300-page document showed its first page after 5.6 s before and 5.9 s after in the browser: pdf.js answers
page requests from its worker, and the wait is pdf.js itself arriving. Reverted in the same branch.
