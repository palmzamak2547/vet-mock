# 5.165.0: the atlas gains a whole cat — skeleton and muscle layer

The atlas's cat coverage was a skull only. Two new whole-body specimens from westerly's feline
studies (Sketchfab, CC BY 4.0) fill it out: the **feline skeleton** (`feline-skeleton-westerly`)
and the **feline ecorche** (`feline-ecorche-westerly`, the muscle layer). Students orbit and zoom
each body and compare them against the other specimens; the collection now lists 8 selectable
examples across 3 species.

- **One surface, no invented parts.** Both source OBJ exports arrive split at the 65,535-vertex
  boundary into whole-body chunks (eight for the skeleton, five for the ecorche). The chunks are
  export fragments, not anatomy, so the assembly (`scripts/assemble-atlas-feline.mjs`, keyed by
  input SHA-256) merges them into one surface with every source triangle kept, and both ship as
  `kind: 'whole'` like the Edinburgh skulls — nothing is presented as a selectable bone or a
  named muscle.
- **Honest posture and provenance.** The author's standing pose is kept (display rotation
  `0,0,0`); the scope text says both are artist sculpts — the skeleton is the author's base for
  an ecorche study — not scans, with no breed, age or sex, and that the ecorche is not registered
  to the skeleton. The CC BY credit rides inside the published GLB bytes and in
  `public/atlas/ATTRIBUTION.md`; each specimen carries its provenance and source manifest.
- **A third model was rejected.** Pixelbeaker's *Normal Feline Anatomy* reports
  `isDownloadable: false` on the Sketchfab API, so it cannot pass through the provenance
  pipeline; the acquisition record in `docs/veterinary-atlas-full-body.md` notes it as rejected
  rather than pulled from someone else's deployment.
- Weights: skeleton quick 36.5k / detail 56.9k triangles, ecorche quick 39.8k / detail 96.2k
  triangles — on-demand files, outside the app shell.
