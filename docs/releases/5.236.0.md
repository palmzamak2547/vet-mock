# 5.236.0: long question text is typeset, not dumped line by line

On 15 Nov the owner sent two iPad pictures (landscape) of an Equine POA question and asked why the question text
read so oddly. A stem is stored as the seniors recorded it, plain lines of text, and `RichText` turned each line
break into a `<br>`: in 209588 the case, a heading-like line (`Flexion test`), the result lines, the lead-in
sentences and the question itself were sixteen equal lines with the question last; in 209599 twenty-one lab values
ran down the card as ragged text with a literal `|`, and the row holding a glossary term (`HCT`) was taller than its
neighbours.

## The rule

`src/lib/rich-blocks.js` (the layer that already cut a passage or an explanation into prose and tables) now also cuts
a run of prose into typed blocks, `proseBlocks`. Every block carries offsets in the text, and the offsets meet: joined,
the slices give the text back character for character, and each line's parts give the line. Kinds:

- `para`: one or more lines; a blank line ends it.
- `pairs`: 3 or more lines `label: value | range`, or 3 or more `label: value`, never mixed.
- `results`: 2 or more lines that end in a result in brackets (`Fetlock (Negative)`); the bracket holds a result word
  (negative, positive, normal, ปกติ, พบ ...), so a sentence ending in a remark in brackets stays a sentence.
- `list`: 2 or more lines opening with the same kind of marker (`1.` `1)` `ก.` `a)` `-` `•` `—`). Numbers must count on
  by one (`4.ตรวจ` with no space still counts), letters must start at `a`, `A` or `ก`; a run that breaks the count stays
  plain lines, all of it, so two lines opening with a genus initial (`C. parvum`) are not a list. A line that carries on
  from the one above (`→ ...`) never leads a block.
- A short line directly above a `pairs` or `results` block, or a sentence there ending in ดังนี้, ดังต่อไปนี้ or `:`, is
  its `lead`.

When in doubt a line is `para`, and a wrong guess costs nothing: the words are the same either way.

`splitStem` decides the ask: a stem with 4 or more non-empty lines whose last line is not part of a run and asks (a
question mark or a question word read as a word, at most 200 characters) and whose first line does not also ask is cut;
the last line alone is the heading the card shows, and everything above it is the stem body in body type. A stem that
asks first and gives its case after, or that ends on its data, is not cut: a paragraph of findings must not become the
heading. The cut stems are pinned by id in the unit test, so a new one is a decision someone reads. Every other stem
draws exactly as before.

## What changed

- `src/components/RichBlocks.jsx`: `ProseBlocks` draws the blocks (paragraphs 0.6em apart instead of an empty line; a
  `pairs` grid of label, value and range with tabular figures, the `|` kept in the page for a screen reader and the
  column gap standing for it on screen; `results` with the bracket in its own column; `list` with a hanging marker; a
  `lead` tight on its block, in medium weight only when it is a short label of 40 characters or fewer; a sentence that leads in stays regular, so nothing the paper did not emphasise reads as a clue). `RichBlocks` typesets the prose of every explanation it draws, and the
  passage in the review list. `StemText` and `SplitStem` are the one helper every stem surface uses.
- Surfaces: the question card (`Question.jsx`), the review list and results review (`ReviewView.jsx`), the spaced
  repetition card (`SRSessionView.jsx`), the daily question (`TodaysQModal.jsx`), the race (`RaceView.jsx`) and the printed question paper
  (`QuestionPaper.jsx`, plain text as before). The share card and the list rows keep their text.
- Reading width: paragraphs, leads and lists hold a 36em measure; a grid takes only the width it needs, and a lab grid
  of 8 or more rows sits in two columns side by side where its frame is 42em or wider (an iPad in landscape), one
  column on a phone. No cell is coloured: which value is out of range is the student's call.
- `TermLinkedRichText.jsx`: a glossary term is a button, an atomic inline box, so its 2px padding made its line 4px
  taller than the next. A matching negative margin gives the line its height back and keeps the hit area.
- The read-aloud keeps marking words in a cut stem: the element it reads from is the whole stem, body and ask.

## Counts (whole bank, `tests/unit/prose-blocks.test.mjs`)

9 stems are cut (209588, 209599, 211082 and six OSCE stems that end on their question); 4,350 explanations and, in
the review list only, 62 passages draw as blocks. Every other stem renders the same markup as before.

## Left as it was

- The passage pane on the card (`SmartPassage`) still draws its prose as one run: a student's pen strokes on it are
  stored as positions on the page, and re-flowing the text would move the ink off its words.
- Questions that ask first and give the case after (the Equine POA case series 209585 to 209622) keep their stem whole.
