## P0 — a student is being misled right now

### 1. Final-exam topics appeared under กลางภาค ✅ fixed, not yet committed

`TopicSelectView` never read `topic.examScope`, and the study catalog has no
scope logic at all, so choosing กลางภาค listed all ten equine reproduction
topics. The five marked `final` came up empty and printed "รอเนื้อหาเพิ่ม",
which tells a student the content is coming when the truth is that the topic is
not on their paper.

**A wrong turn worth keeping.** The first fix also kept a topic when it *held*
questions scoped to this paper. That reads as generous and is wrong: a question's
`examScope` is the paper it was **recorded from**, and many of those papers
belong to another cohort — the single midterm question under equine pregnancy is
sourced "บันทึกหลังสอบของรุ่นพี่ Vet 85", and the ten under equine dentistry are
"รวบรวมโดยรุ่นพี่ Vet 85". A topic's `examScope` is **this** year's timetable;
the entries carry "(Course Schedule 2026)" beside them. Only the topic's own
scope can answer "is this on my paper". Pinned by `tests/e2e/topic-scope.spec.js`.

### 2. The same leak everywhere else ⬜ not started

Palm: "ดูทุกวิชาครับ จุดอื่นๆด้วย". `questionInScope` is called in exactly one
place, `App.jsx:503`. Every other surface that lists topics, counts or resources
has to be checked for the same omission.

### 3. A matching question showed no answer ✅ fixed, needs a look with eyes

`MatchDragDrop` rendered the student's own choice and a ✗, and the banner said
"ถูกบางคู่ — ดูเฉลยด้านล่าง". `pair.right` was never rendered anywhere, so a
student who got three of four pairs wrong could not learn the right pairing from
the page that told them to look for it. The answer now sits on the row it
belongs to and the banner says where it is.

### 4. True/False reads as both right and wrong at once ⬜ not started

Palm: "ภาษามันแปลกๆไหม … คนจะสับสนไหม ว่าสรุปถูกหรือผิด". On a correct answer
the chosen button is **red**, the banner says **✓ ถูกต้อง!**, and the
explanation directly under it opens with **ไม่ถูกต้อง** — which is about the
statement, not the student. Three signals, two of them saying the opposite of
what happened.
